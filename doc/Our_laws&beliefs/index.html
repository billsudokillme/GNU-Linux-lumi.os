Our_laws&beliefs

In short, Lumi’s purpose and goals for improvement center on being a free and open-source system that respects user privacy. (This is why Waterfox is included as an alternative to Firefox due to Mozilla's policies, though Firefox remains available since some users prefer it). It aims to deliver the lowest possible memory and resource consumption while providing a good selection of software, minimal usage complexity, and the flexibility to handle issues with little to no user intervention in most cases. You will find many applications you might need right out of the box, though you will almost certainly install additional programs for your work, studies, personal use, or whatever your reason for using the system may be. Although the system does not aim to offer an overwhelming number of choices—such as multiple desktop environments or an abundance of tools you might never need (since our team, or rather myself alone at the time of writing this, prefers focusing on fewer features with high quality over getting distracted across many programs without making any of them truly great)—it strives to give you the maximum amount of customization within what is available. Ultimately, the system does not claim to be "perfect" in all of these aspects; rather, it aims to be solid across the board, prioritizing lightweight size and low resource usage over pure user convenience where necessary.

Additionally, whoever is responsible for developing Lumi (such as myself) must not accept any paid sponsorship or support from a for-profit organization. This is to avoid corporate control and to prevent what happened to Ubuntu. However, this is permitted if the system's survival is threatened (i.e., the project faces being frozen); in that case, if no other solution exists, green light is given to sign sponsorship agreements with for-profit companies.

— Youssef Ghouari
